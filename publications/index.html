  1. Optimal design and techno-economic analysis of wave-integrated hybrid energy systems for coastal communities
    Oluwabunmi Iwakin, Mehregan Gilani Nejad, Daniela Villacres, and 3 more authors
    Energy Conversion and Management, 2027
    Abstract DOI

    Wave energy has the potential to improve renewable complementarity in coastal microgrids; however, its technoeconomic competitiveness under moderate resource conditions remains uncertain. This study develops a two-stage stochastic mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) framework to evaluate the integration of wave energy converters (WECs) into hybrid offshore microgrids under joint resource and demand uncertainty. Renewable generation and demand are modeled using ARIMA-based forecasting, with interdependencies captured via a Student-t copula and reduced through k-medoids clustering. The framework optimally sizes wind, floating photovoltaics, battery energy storage, and WEC systems by minimizing the expected annual system cost. A case study in coastal New Jersey indicates that, under current cost assumptions, WEC deployment is not economically viable, despite the potential to reduce storage requirements through resource complementarity. Sensitivity analysis identifies capital cost reduction thresholds of approximately 70%–80%, depending on device type and operating policy, to achieve cost parity and system-level benefits. Demand-side management improves curtailment outcomes but provides limited economic gains without substantial WEC cost reductions. Results demonstrate that the capital cost, rather than resource variability, is the primary barrier to wave integration in moderate Atlantic wave climates. The proposed framework provides a generalizable tool for evaluating deployment thresholds of emerging marine technologies within multi-resource energy systems under uncertainty.

  1. Deep learning-enhanced economic dispatch for integrated microgrid-water systems with desalination
    Oluwabunmi Iwakin and Faegheh Moazeni
    Journal of Water Process Engineering, 2026
    Abstract DOI

    The impacts of climate change and the rapid depletion of freshwater resources underscore the urgent need for the exploitation of alternative water and energy resources to ensure the sustainable operation and management of the critical infrastructure associated with them. This paper proposes a mixed integer nonlinear programming economic dispatch model for the joint operation and management of integrated water-microgrid systems, while accounting for maintenance, reliability and environmental constraints within a unified framework. This study further tackles the associated computational complexity that might arise from solving such optimization problems, particularly for large systems, by developing a multichannel convolutional neural network model for realtime economic dispatch solutions under different operational conditions. The case studies analyzed in this work demonstrate that, under specific conditions, investments in maintaining battery energy storage system charge availability at the end of each day can lead to substantial operational and emissions cost savings, reaching up to $8.4/day for the system under consideration. The developed data-driven approach also highlights the role of historical dispatch information on providing contextual insight offering more-informed future scheduling decisions, and with up to 94% in performance improvements from baseline models.

  2. Optimal Scheduling for Water-Energy Systems Based on Data-Driven Learned Constraints
    Oluwabunmi Iwakin, Javad Khazaei, and Faegheh Moazeni
    In World Environmental and Water Resources Congress, 2026
    Abstract DOI

    The strong interdependence between water and energy systems is primarily accounted for in the utilization of dispatched energy for the operation of pumps. The optimal utilization of water resources and the scheduling of pumps could provide substantial energy and financial cost savings of up to 23%. However, the optimal scheduling for water distribution systems remains a challenging nonconvex mixed-integer nonlinear programming problem. Existing approaches often rely on heuristics, which lack guarantees of global optimality, or adopt linear approximations that introduce numerous auxiliary variables and significantly increase computational burden. To address this challenge, we propose a novel linearization framework for the original MINLP problem that directly embeds machine-learning models trained to learn system constraints into the optimization formulation. We leverage the ability of machine learning models to learn complex nonlinear relationships between variables and their representability in mixed-integer optimization, including linear regression and multilayer perceptron neural networks, to linearize dynamic headloss and pump head curve relationships. We demonstrate the applicability of this approach using two small water distribution networks connected with a microgrid. We show that the presented approach offers competitive computational efficiency and improved solution quality compared to existing methods.

  3. InTaSet: A benchmark dataset for data-driven system identification and fault detection in an interconnected water system
    Saskia A Putri, Daniela Villacres, Nazia Raza, and 2 more authors
    In World Environmental and Water Resources Congress, 2026
    Abstract DOI Dataset

    Data-driven methods for dynamic model identification, forecasting, and fault detection in water systems research have received significant attention. However, validating these learning-based methods remains a challenge. Simulation-generated datasets, although easily accessible, rely on simplified disturbance descriptions and lack sensor drift. As a result, models trained may fail to transfer reliably to real-world physical systems. Simultaneously, physical laboratory-scale testbeds are expensive to build and rarely available to the broader community. This limits researchers’ ability to benchmark new algorithms for diverse applications under realistic hydraulic dynamics. This work bridges the gap by introducing the Interconnected Tank Dataset (InTaSet), a carefully curated, publicly available benchmark dataset generated from a laboratory-scale quadruple-tank system. The dataset provides (1) training and testing datasets for system identification purposes under four separate experimental setups and (2) various sensor fault scenarios to mimic sensor drift in real-world systems. The dataset is equipped with (1) real-world sensor noise and actuator imperfections with high resolution at 0.1 s time interval, (2) rich excitation and multiple operating modes, and (3) a unified structure designed to evaluate a wide range of applications for data-driven identification, prediction, and fault-detection algorithms. The interconnected and reconfigurable quadruple-tank system exhibits cross-coupling and nonlinear dynamics, offering a compelling testbed for validating data-driven methods. Researchers can utilize the data to test algorithms offline, ensuring fair and reproducible comparisons. To support fair comparison across methods, we include a Neural ODE-based system identification baseline developed using the InTaSet data.

  4. An Uncertainty-Aware Leakage Detection Framework Based on Monte Carlo Dropout Neural Network
    Oluwabunmi Iwakin, Nazia Raza, and Faegheh Moazeni
    In World Environmental and Water Resources Congress, 2026
    Abstract DOI

    Leakage is a critical challenge in water distribution systems, leading to significant water and economic losses. Traditional detection methods are either invasive (costly and impractical for large networks) or non-invasive, including hardware-based, model-based, and data-driven approaches. While hardware solutions are expensive and model-based approaches are computationally intensive, data-driven methods offer a more scalable alternative. However, their deterministic predictions provide no quantification of uncertainty, limiting their reliability for real-time decision-making. To address this gap, we propose a novel, non-invasive leakage detection framework based on a Monte Carlo Dropout (MCD) neural network. An MCD model integrates Monte Carlo sampling with dropout regularization, enabling both uncertainty quantification and overfitting prevention. By using predictive distributions rather than single outputs, we quantify epistemic uncertainties and establish robust detection thresholds, ensuring that leakage detection explicitly incorporates confidence levels in the model’s predictions. We evaluate the framework on the Net3 benchmark network using EPANET simulations to generate normal and leakage events. The MCD model is trained solely on leak-free data, enabling it to detect anomalies when leakage occurs during inference. Results show that incorporating uncertainty intervals ensures detection reliability, reduces false alarms, and supports risk-aware operational decisions. This framework offers a scalable, real-time, software-based solution for uncertainty-informed leakage detection in modern water utilities.

  5. WDSEventDB: A Real-Time Benchmark Dataset for Event Diagnosis in Water Distribution Networks
    Nazia Raza, Oluwabunmi Iwakin, Daniela Villacres, and 2 more authors
    In World Environmental and Water Resources Congress, 2026
    Abstract DOI Dataset

    Water distribution networks (WDS) are critical infrastructures whose security against stress and failures is vital to societal well-being and socio-economic development. Recent technological advances in sensor technologies have enabled the collection of real-time hydraulic datasets. These datasets can be leveraged to develop data-driven models for real-time event detection in WDS. However, most existing datasets remain proprietary and inaccessible to the public. Moreover, publicly available databases are limited in scope, as they focus on a single event type (e.g., leakages) and rely on hydraulic simulations. To address these limitations, we introduce a WDS Event Diagnosis Benchmark (WDSEventDB), a publicly available real-time sensor dataset designed to support the development of event-detection frameworks for diverse anomalies within WDS, including cyber-physical attacks, leakages, and sensor failures. The dataset is curated in real-time from a lab-scale WDS by emulating failures. We also provide four baseline detection models to facilitate benchmarking and advance research in WDS.

  6. When MILP Beats QP: Piecewise-Linear Reformulations of Sequentially Coupled Bilinear Programs
    Quentin Ploussard, Maris Usis, Oluwabunmi Iwakin, and 1 more author
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.27312, 2026
    Abstract DOI

    The presence of bilinear terms in mathematical modeling generally yields nonconvex quadratic programs (QPs) that remain computationally challenging to solve to global optimality. While continuous piecewise linear (CPWL) approximations can reformulate these nonlinearities into mixed-integer linear programs (MILPs), the geometric construction of the domain partition heavily dictates the resulting solver efficiency. In this paper, we present efficient MILP formulations for approximating bilinear terms and rigorously evaluate their computational merits against direct QP solvers. First, we introduce CPWL approximations with arbitrary high degrees of accuracy that explicitly account for and exploit the inherent symmetries of the unit bilinear function. Second, we analytically establish the exact maximum and average approximation errors of the CPWL approximations. Third, we construct and compare three distinct MILP formulations (a “Triangle”, a “Square”, and a difference-of-convex “DC” formulation) of the CPWL functions. Fourth, we formalize a highly relevant class of optimization models, Sequentially Coupled Bilinear Programs (SCBP), where variables represent system states and state changes. Finally, through extensive numerical experiments, we demonstrate that our compact “Square” MILP formulation achieves superior computational performance on SCBP instances with long sequence horizons, allowing mature open-source MILP solvers to effectively outperform QP solvers.

  1. Rapid characterization of MSW and RDF feedstocks for waste-to-energy process using LIBS and ML techniques
    Jincheng Liu, Oluwabunmi Iwakin, Carlos E Romero, and 5 more authors
    Waste Management, 2025
    Abstract DOI

    The heterogeneity in the composition of municipal solid wastes (MSW) poses significant challenges in the production of biofuel and bioproducts. This research aims to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of waste analysis and characterization by introducing a fast characterization approach for MSW-derived refuse-derived fuels (RDF) by combining Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy (LIBS) with advanced machine learning (ML) techniques. The approach combines data pre-processing of LIBS spectra of RDF, and the development of ML models trained on domain and theory-based spectral features for predicting process parameters. These models are adept at predicting key process parameters like High Heating Value (HHV), carbon content, and volatile matter. This approach can achieve an average RRMSE of 2.13% and R2 of 0.98 or higher for all considered parameters on testing data. This work demonstrates significant potential for improving waste sorting, processing efficiency, and environmental compliance over traditional labor- and time-intensive laboratory waste analysis and characterization.

  2. Hardware-in-the-Loop Simulation of Data-Driven Economic Dispatch Problem for Integrated Water–Energy Systems
    Oluwabunmi M Iwakin and Faegheh Moazeni
    In World Environmental and Water Resources Congress, 2025
    Abstract DOI

    Integrating smart water and energy systems introduces substantial complexities, prompting the development of more advanced algorithms and techniques for their economic and efficient operation. However, applying these algorithms in real-world scenarios, particularly for (near-) real-time economic dispatch problems, presents notable computational challenges. This underscores the need for validated solutions that minimize computational complexity while optimizing power scheduling for water distribution systems. To address this, the present work employs machine learning (ML) models to solve integrated water–energy economic dispatch problems in the shortest possible time. A hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) water distribution system (WDS) testbed is used for data generation and model validation, while a conventional deterministic optimization algorithm is used to obtain the final training data for the ML model. The testbed’s integration of WDS hardware and software components facilitates iterative testing and validation of developed models and algorithms. The findings include reliable real-time solutions to the economic dispatch problem with reduced computational time, showcasing its potential for real-world implementation.

  3. Stochastic Economic Dispatch for Combined Water–Energy Microgrid Systems Considering Data-Driven Scenario Generation Approach
    Oluwabunmi M Iwakin, Faegheh Moazeni, and Javad Khazaei
    In World Environmental and Water Resources Congress, 2025
    Abstract DOI

    Numerous bilateral opportunities exist within the water–energy nexus, leading to an increased focus on the efficient operation of combined water–energy microgrid systems. Emphasis is being placed on developing methods that prescribe the most economical and efficient use of distributed energy resources and the design of energy-efficient water systems. However, achieving this objective is often complicated by significant uncertainties in the availability and variability of renewable energy sources, along with fluctuations in water demand, which introduce complexities into the optimization process and impact the reliability of traditional deterministic models. This work develops a k-means algorithm with a scenario reduction method for scenario generation to solve a two-stage stochastic economic dispatch problem. The algorithm is tested for Telford Borough in Pennsylvania. The proposed framework provides reliable economic power dispatch for water supply systems while accounting for uncertainties in future energy demands, which are directly linked to fluctuating water demand. The study demonstrates the advantages of employing a stochastic scheduling approach while comparing it to the deterministic power dispatch results. The findings demonstrate the effectiveness of the method in enhancing operational stability, achieving up to 12% operational cost savings. They also highlight the importance of accurate forecasting for robust energy dispatch in large-scale integrated water–energy microgrid systems.

  4. Data-driven economic dispatch towards operational management of distributed energy resources for grid-connected water-energy microgrids
    Oluwabunmi Iwakin, Faegheh Moazeni, and Javad Khazaei
    Energy, Aug 2025
    Abstract DOI

    This paper addresses the critical need for economic dispatch optimization in integrated water-microgrid systems by introducing innovative data-driven techniques to overcome computational challenges. Leveraging machine learning models, we achieved rapid resolution of objective functions and constraints in water–energy system dispatch optimization. It effectively tackles the computational challenges associated with dynamic economic dispatch problems in these nonlinear, interdependent, complex systems, showcasing remarkable improvements with computational speed enhancements of over 10⁴ times compared to conventional numerical optimization methods. The accuracy of the machine-learning algorithms is demonstrated through the superior dispatch efficiency of the developed data-driven models for solving the economic dispatch problem, with approximately 99% accuracy for all dispatch predictions for the gradient boosting model. The modeling errors, however, are more pronounced with variations in water demand and grid connection profiles. Furthermore, the SHAP explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) technique is applied to interpret the model predictions and input–output relationships. Notably, the presented work enables the incorporation of offline data-driven systems for in-depth analysis of the impacts of dispatch decisions, thereby enhancing system robustness. This innovative approach holds significant promise for achieving cost-effective and sustainable power generation in line with global zero-carbon emission targets.

  5. Artificial Intelligence Techniques in LIBS Data Analysis and Interpretation
    Oluwabunmi Iwakin, Jincheng Liu, Joe Craparo, and 5 more authors
    In Laser Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy (LIBS) Chemometrics, Environmental and Forensic Applications, 2025
    Abstract DOI

    Laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy (LIBS) remains widely utilized in various scientific and industrial applications for its fast, in situ, quasi-nondestructive analysis of materials. However, the size and complexity of the datasets generated through LIBS necessitate efficient approaches for their handling and interpretation. This chapter examines the application of artificial intelligence (AI) techniques to manage large volumes of multidimensional LIBS data and enhance analytical processes, including data analysis and interpretation while highlighting its role in improving real-time analysis and decision-making. Focusing on waste-to-energy applications, this chapter demonstrates how AI models, including support vector regression (SVR) and random forest (RF), streamline the prediction of critical waste properties such as proximate and ash elemental analyses and heating values. These models and cross-validation techniques ensure high generalization ability for LIBS qualitative/quantitative analyses, demonstrating the transformative potential of AI in optimizing industrial workflows and driving sustainable technological innovations.

  6. Machine Learning in the Context of Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy
    Jincheng Liu, Oluwabunmi Iwakin, Liang Cheng, and 3 more authors
    In Laser Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy (LIBS) Chemometrics, Environmental and Forensic Applications, 2025
    Abstract DOI

    This chapter explores the integration of machine learning (ML) with Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy (LIBS), a powerful technique for elemental analysis. The combination of ML and LIBS enhances data processing, enabling more precise material classification, elemental concentration predictions, and complex dataset interpretation. The chapter begins by introducing the fundamental concepts of machine learning, including supervised, unsupervised, and deep learning methods, and their applications within the context of LIBS. Key supervised machine learning techniques such as decision trees, random forests, support vector machines, and neural networks are analyzed, highlighting their roles in improving LIBS accuracy and efficiency. The chapter also emphasizes the importance of data splitting and feature selection to optimize model performance. Special attention is given to advanced methods like Gaussian process regression and self-organizing maps, which offer probabilistic and unsupervised approaches to spectral data analysis. The chapter concludes by addressing the ongoing challenges in applying machine learning to LIBS, including computational demands and model interpretability, while outlining the potential future advancements that will further enhance the analytical capabilities of LIBS.

  1. Improving urban water demand forecast using conformal prediction-based hybrid machine learning models
    Oluwabunmi Iwakin and Faegheh Moazeni
    Journal of Water Process Engineering, 2024
    Abstract DOI

    This paper presents a probabilistic forecasting method that predicts the future water consumption patterns, taking into account the inherent uncertainty in the system. The proposed method leverages statistical techniques to estimate the probability distribution of future water demand scenarios. The findings of this study will provide decision-makers with a range of possible alternatives that facilitate better planning and management of water resources. We developed a conformal prediction-based hybrid demand forecast model, which combines convolutional neural network (CNN) and bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) while comparing it with other machine learning models for probabilistic hourly water demand forecasting. Additionally, we address crucial considerations when implementing a probabilistic forecasting system, including selecting appropriate data and choosing model parameters. The performance of the proposed model is validated for probabilistic water demand forecasting in real-world settings. Results indicate noteworthy improvement in deterministic and probabilistic predictions by 10 % and 26.7 %, respectively. The findings underscore the potential benefits of this approach for improved decision-making and resource management. The results also demonstrate that the proposed hybrid model outperforms the compared machine learning models with better prediction performance and increased robustness for handling prediction uncertainties.

  1. Short-term water demand prediction using machine learning techniques—a case study of telford borough in pennsylvania
    Oluwabunmi M Iwakin and Faegheh Moazeni
    In World Environmental and Water Resources Congress, 2023
    Abstract DOI

    Increasing global populations, changing water consumption patterns, and climate change are continuously putting a strain on available water resources. Identifying these changes in consumption patterns and future demand requirements through water demand forecasting plays a key role in the allocation, planning, and management of urban water resources and infrastructure. In the last decades, advances in statistics, machine learning, and artificial intelligence techniques have shown promising results in predicting future water demands. This study aims to forecast hourly water demand, while factoring the effects of other exogenous features on consumption patterns using machine learning techniques for Telford Borough, a predominantly residential town in Pennsylvania, US. In this study, the predictive abilities of the support vector machine and the random forest regression models have been compared in forecasting water demand using past demand, and temporal and climatic features. The results demonstrated the practicability of applying machine learning solutions to water management with random forest being the best model, and temporal features being the most important in the exploratory variables. The authors conclude that these predictive models can be used for near real-time forecasting to guide the allocation and planning decisions of water resources.
